DEPARTMENT OF ENVIRONMENTAL PROTECTION
Tree Preservation Ordinance Policy
Document No. DEP-POL-019           Effective Date: September 1, 2024
STATUS: CURRENT

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. PURPOSE

Governs the city's protected/heritage tree designation program under
City Code §23-1, covering both right-of-way and certain qualifying
private-property trees (large diameter, native species, or
historically significant). This is DISTINCT from DPW's routine
right-of-way tree MAINTENANCE role under DPW Urban Forestry Policy — DEP
determines protected STATUS and approves or denies removal of a
protected tree; DPW performs the physical maintenance/removal work once
approved.

2. SCOPE

  2.1  REMOVAL REQUEST FOR A PROTECTED TREE (by a property owner or after
       a hazard assessment): Requires DEP review and approval, target
       determination within 15 business days, faster if DPW's parallel
       hazard assessment (see DPW Urban Forestry Policy 2.3) confirms an
       immediate safety risk.

  2.2  SUSPECTED UNAUTHORIZED REMOVAL OR DAMAGE TO A PROTECTED TREE
       (citizen reports a protected tree was cut down or visibly damaged
       without apparent permit): MEDIUM priority, investigated within 10
       business days, may result in a citation and required replacement
       planting.

  2.3  A general "this tree looks unhealthy, can someone check it"
       report about a right-of-way tree with NO indication of protected
       status is typically a DPW Urban Forestry matter first — DPW
       determines whether protected-status review is even needed.

3. RELATED POLICIES

See also DPW Urban Forestry Policy — the two departments' roles are
sequential and complementary on a hazardous protected tree, not
competing.
