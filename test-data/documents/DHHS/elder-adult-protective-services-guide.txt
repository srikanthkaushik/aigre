DEPARTMENT OF HEALTH AND HUMAN SERVICES
Elder & Adult Protective Services Guide
Document No. DHHS-POL-006          Effective Date: August 1, 2023
STATUS: CURRENT

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. PURPOSE

Governs intake and response for citizen reports expressing concern about
an elderly or dependent adult who may be experiencing self-neglect,
neglect by a caregiver, financial exploitation, or abuse.

2. INTAKE

  2.1  Reports may be submitted by a concerned neighbor, family member, or
       any citizen — the reporter need not be a mandatory reporter under
       state law, though certain professionals (home health workers,
       facility staff) are mandatory reporters under separate state
       statute referenced in DHHS Mandatory Reporting & Escalation Guide.

  2.2  Default classification is HIGH priority (vulnerable-population
       impact). Classification escalates to CRITICAL if the report
       describes an immediate danger indicator (e.g., "he hasn't answered
       the door or phone in three days," "she has visible injuries and
       is afraid to call for help").

3. RESPONSE TIMELINE

  3.1  HIGH priority: in-person welfare check or phone contact attempt
       within 24 hours.

  3.2  CRITICAL priority: welfare check dispatched same day, coordinated
       with police if forced entry or an immediate safety concern is
       indicated.

4. DISTINGUISHING FROM A NOISE OR NUISANCE COMPLAINT

A report framed as "my neighbor is a nuisance" or "my neighbor's dog
barks all night" is NOT an Adult Protective Services matter merely
because it involves a neighbor — route noise/nuisance complaints to DEP
under the Noise Ordinance Enforcement Policy. This guide applies
specifically to concern for the person's own safety or well-being.

5. RELATED POLICIES

See also DHHS Mandatory Reporting & Escalation Guide, and Citywide
Escalation & Safety-Flag Policy.
