DEPARTMENT OF HEALTH AND HUMAN SERVICES
Benefits Eligibility & Appeals FAQ
Last updated: December 2025

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q: My application for city-administered emergency assistance benefits was
denied. Can I appeal?

A: Yes. Submit an appeal through the grievance portal under "DHHS —
Benefits Appeal" within 30 calendar days of the denial notice. Include
your case number from the denial letter. DHHS Benefits Appeals Unit will
schedule a review within 10 business days of receiving your appeal.

Q: How long does a benefits eligibility appeal take to resolve?

A: Standard appeals are resolved within 30 business days of the review
hearing. Appeals citing an emergency circumstance (imminent loss of
housing, medical necessity) may request expedited review, resolved within
10 business days, at the discretion of the Appeals Unit.

Q: Can I check the status of my appeal through the portal?

A: Yes, the portal shows high-level status (Received / Under Review /
Decision Issued) but does not display the substance of your case file,
consistent with the Citywide Privacy & PII Handling Notice. For case
details, contact the Benefits Appeals Unit directly.

Q: Does DHHS handle disputes about state-administered benefits (SNAP,
Medicaid)?

A: No. This FAQ and the grievance portal's DHHS category cover only
city-administered emergency assistance programs. State-administered
benefit programs have their own appeals process through the state agency
and are out of scope for this portal — see Citywide Jurisdiction &
Out-of-Scope Guide.

Q: What happens if I miss the 30-day appeal window?

A: Late appeals may be accepted for good cause (e.g., documented medical
incapacity during the window) at the Appeals Unit's discretion, but there
is no guaranteed SLA for late-appeal review.
