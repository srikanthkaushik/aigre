Standard Operating Procedure — Road Surface Maintenance

1. PURPOSE

Pursuant to City Code §14-2 ("Maintenance of Public Roadways"), the
Department of Transportation ("DOT") is responsible for the repair of
road-surface defects on all city-maintained roadways, including but not
limited to potholes, surface cracking, and pavement heaving.

2. SCOPE

This SOP applies to road-surface defects reported through the citizen
grievance portal, by phone to the DOT Customer Service Line, or referred
from another department. It does not apply to sidewalks (see DPW Sidewalk
Maintenance Policy), state-maintained highways, or private roadways.

3. RESPONSE TIMELINE

  3.1  Upon receipt of a citizen-reported pothole, DOT shall dispatch a
       field inspector within three (3) business days to confirm the
       defect and assign a severity classification (Minor / Moderate /
       Severe) per Table 1.

  3.2  Repair of a confirmed pothole shall be completed within ten (10)
       business days of the initial citizen report, regardless of
       severity classification, subject to weather and material
       availability exceptions under Section 4.

Table 1 — Severity Classification (informational only; does not affect
repair timeline under this revision)
  Minor    | Surface depth < 2 in.  | Cosmetic / low traffic impact
  Moderate | Surface depth 2-4 in.  | Vehicle damage risk
  Severe   | Surface depth > 4 in.  | Immediate hazard, see escalation policy

4. EXCEPTIONS

Repair timelines under Section 3.2 may be extended in the event of (a)
sustained sub-freezing temperatures preventing asphalt cure, (b) declared
weather emergencies, or (c) supply chain disruption affecting patching
material, each subject to supervisor sign-off and citizen notification.

5. RELATED POLICIES

  See also DOT Traffic Signal Repair & Timing Policy for signal-adjacent
  road defects, and DPW Sidewalk Maintenance Policy (Policy 4.2) for
  defects on pedestrian infrastructure.
