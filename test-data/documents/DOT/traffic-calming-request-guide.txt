DEPARTMENT OF TRANSPORTATION
Traffic Calming Request Guide
Last updated: April 2025

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q: My street has a lot of speeding traffic. Can the city install speed
bumps?

A: Traffic calming (speed bumps, raised crosswalks, curb extensions) is a
resident-initiated PLANNING request, not a complaint — submit through the
portal under "DOT — Traffic Calming Request." It is not governed by the
standard SLA table; instead, DOT conducts a traffic study (typically
6-10 weeks) to confirm the street qualifies under the Traffic Calming
Policy's volume/speed thresholds before any installation is scheduled.

Q: Does a traffic calming request need neighbor support?

A: Yes, DOT requires a petition signed by a majority of residents on the
affected block before conducting the traffic study, to confirm broad
support before committing study resources.

Q: My request was denied because the street didn't meet the volume
threshold. Can I appeal?

A: Yes, through the Citywide Grievance Appeals Process, though traffic
calming denials are reviewed on their technical merits (the traffic study
data) rather than under the standard case-reopening framework.

Q: Is this different from reporting an actual pothole or road hazard?

A: Yes — a specific defect (pothole, damaged sign) should be reported as
a standard complaint under the relevant DOT SOP, which has a tracked SLA.
A traffic calming request is a proactive infrastructure change request,
not a defect report.
