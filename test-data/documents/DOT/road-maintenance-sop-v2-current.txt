CITY DEPARTMENT OF TRANSPORTATION
Standard Operating Procedure — Road Surface Maintenance
Document No. DOT-SOP-014 Rev. B    Effective Date: January 15, 2026
STATUS: CURRENT. Supersedes DOT-SOP-014 (effective March 1, 2023).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. PURPOSE

Pursuant to City Code §14-2 ("Maintenance of Public Roadways"), the
Department of Transportation ("DOT") is responsible for the repair of
road-surface defects on all city-maintained roadways, including but not
limited to potholes, surface cracking, and pavement heaving. This revision
shortens response timelines following the FY2025 Infrastructure
Modernization Ordinance and adds severity-based prioritization.

2. SCOPE

This SOP applies to road-surface defects reported through the citizen
grievance portal, by phone to the DOT Customer Service Line, or referred
from another department. It does not apply to sidewalks (see DPW Sidewalk
Maintenance Policy 4.2), state-maintained highways, or private roadways.
Full road-repaving and resurfacing projects are governed by the DOT
Capital Projects schedule, not this SOP — this SOP covers point-repair of
individual defects only.

3. RESPONSE TIMELINE

  3.1  Upon receipt of a citizen-reported pothole, DOT shall dispatch a
       field inspector within two (2) business days to confirm the defect
       and assign a severity classification (Minor / Moderate / Severe)
       per Table 1.

  3.2  Repair of a confirmed pothole classified Minor or Moderate shall be
       completed within five (5) business days of the initial citizen
       report.

  3.3  Repair of a confirmed pothole classified Severe, or any defect
       meeting the hazard criteria in the Citywide Escalation &
       Safety-Flag Policy, shall be prioritized ahead of the standing
       repair queue and completed as soon as practicable, consistent with
       CRITICAL-priority handling.

Table 1 — Severity Classification
  Minor    | Surface depth < 2 in.  | 5 business days
  Moderate | Surface depth 2-4 in.  | 5 business days
  Severe   | Surface depth > 4 in.  | Escalated, see 3.3

4. EXCEPTIONS

Repair timelines under Section 3.2 may be extended in the event of (a)
sustained sub-freezing temperatures preventing asphalt cure, (b) declared
weather emergencies, or (c) supply chain disruption affecting patching
material, each subject to supervisor sign-off and citizen notification via
the portal status page.

5. RELATED POLICIES

  See also DOT Traffic Signal Repair & Timing Policy for signal-adjacent
  road defects, DPW Sidewalk Maintenance Policy (Policy 4.2) for defects
  on pedestrian infrastructure, and the Citywide Escalation & Safety-Flag
  Policy for hazard criteria that trigger priority handling.
