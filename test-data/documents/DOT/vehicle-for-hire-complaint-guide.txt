DEPARTMENT OF TRANSPORTATION
Vehicle-for-Hire Complaint Guide
Last updated: June 2025

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q: Can I file a complaint about a taxi or rideshare driver through the
grievance portal?

A: Mostly no. Rideshare companies (transportation network companies) are
regulated primarily at the state level, and individual driver conduct is
handled through the company's own support channel. The city's direct
enforcement role is limited to: (a) verifying a vehicle-for-hire operating
license is valid, and (b) investigating a specific licensing violation
(e.g., an unlicensed vehicle operating as a taxi). A general service
complaint — rude driver, bad drop-off location, fare dispute — is NOT
something this portal routes to an investigation; see Citywide
Jurisdiction & Out-of-Scope Guide for the general out-of-scope framing
this guide follows.

Q: What if I suspect a vehicle is operating as an unlicensed taxi?

A: That is a legitimate DOT licensing-enforcement matter. Report the
vehicle description, location, and circumstances through the portal under
"DOT — Vehicle-for-Hire Licensing." DOT Licensing Enforcement reviews
within 10 business days.

Q: A rideshare vehicle nearly hit a pedestrian in a crosswalk. Is that a
city matter?

A: A specific traffic-safety incident (near-miss, collision) should be
reported to police, not the grievance portal, regardless of the vehicle
type involved. If the concern is really about crosswalk/signal
infrastructure at that location, that is separately reportable under DOT
Street Signage & Visibility Policy or DOT Traffic Signal Repair & Timing
Policy.
