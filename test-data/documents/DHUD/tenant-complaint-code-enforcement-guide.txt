DEPARTMENT OF HOUSING AND URBAN DEVELOPMENT
Tenant Complaint & Code Enforcement Guide
Document No. DHUD-POL-003          Effective Date: May 1, 2024
STATUS: CURRENT

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. PURPOSE

Explains tenant rights and DHUD's code-enforcement process for habitability
complaints in city-subsidized housing units, under City Code §22-4
("Habitability Standards") and applicable state tenant-protection law.

2. WHAT DHUD CAN INVESTIGATE

  2.1  Failure of a landlord to maintain heat, hot water, or working
       plumbing in a city-subsidized unit.
  2.2  Pest infestation, mold, or structural hazards within the unit.
  2.3  Retaliation against a tenant for filing a habitability complaint.

3. RESPONSE TIMELINE

  3.1  NO HEAT (during the designated heating season, typically
       October-April) or NO HOT WATER: Classified HIGH priority
       (vulnerable-population impact). DHUD Code Enforcement inspects
       within 24 hours and, if confirmed, issues a correction order to
       the landlord with a 48-hour compliance deadline; failure to comply
       may result in city-arranged emergency heat/water restoration
       billed to the landlord.

  3.2  PEST / MOLD / NON-EMERGENCY HABITABILITY DEFECT: MEDIUM priority,
       inspection within 5 business days.

4. WHAT DHUD DOES NOT HANDLE

Disputes between two private homeowners (property line disputes,
neighbor disagreements not involving a city-subsidized rental unit) are
outside DHUD's jurisdiction — see Citywide Jurisdiction & Out-of-Scope
Guide. Market-rate (non-subsidized) private rentals are also generally
outside DHUD's direct enforcement authority, though tenants may be
referred to the separate city Rental Housing Mediation Program.

5. RELATED POLICIES

See also DHUD Tenant Utility Complaint Guide for the specific process
governing in-unit utility outages, and DPW Water Main Break Response SOP
for city-infrastructure water issues (a different cause requiring a
different department).
