DEPARTMENT OF HOUSING AND URBAN DEVELOPMENT
Rental Housing Mediation Program FAQ
Last updated: April 2025

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q: I have a dispute with my landlord but I don't live in city-subsidized
housing. Can DHUD still help?

A: DHUD's direct code-enforcement authority under the Tenant Complaint &
Code Enforcement Guide applies only to city-subsidized units. For a
market-rate rental dispute (security deposit, lease disagreement, minor
habitability issues not rising to a code violation), the separate Rental
Housing Mediation Program offers voluntary, non-binding mediation between
tenant and landlord.

Q: Is mediation the same as a code-enforcement inspection?

A: No. Mediation is a facilitated conversation with no inspection or
enforcement authority — if the underlying issue is a genuine safety
hazard (no heat, structural damage) even in a market-rate unit, request a
standard habitability inspection referral instead, which DHUD will
conduct regardless of subsidized status when safety is at issue.

Q: How long does mediation take?

A: Sessions are typically scheduled within 15 business days of request;
this is a scheduling process, not an SLA-tracked complaint.

Q: What if my landlord refuses to participate in mediation?

A: Mediation is voluntary for both parties. If your landlord declines,
you may need to pursue the dispute through small claims court or private
legal counsel — this is outside DHUD's authority to compel.
